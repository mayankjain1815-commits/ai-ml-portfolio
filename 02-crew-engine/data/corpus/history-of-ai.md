# A Very Short History of AI

The term "artificial intelligence" was coined in 1956 at the Dartmouth Summer
Research Project on Artificial Intelligence, organized by John McCarthy.
Marvin Minsky and others spent the late 1950s and 1960s building the first
logic-based programs, a period now called the symbolic AI era.

In the 1980s, expert systems became commercially successful. These encoded
human expertise as if-then rules — for example, a medical expert system knew
hundreds of symptoms and diagnoses. But expert systems were brittle: they
needed a human to write every rule, and they could not learn from data.

The big shift began in the late 1990s and 2000s with machine learning.
Instead of writing rules, programmers wrote training algorithms that learned
rules from examples. By 2012, deep neural networks trained on graphics cards
repeatedly won image-recognition competitions, marking the start of the deep
learning era.

In 2017, a landmark paper titled "Attention Is All You Need" introduced the
transformer architecture. Transformers process whole sequences in parallel
and use attention to weigh which words matter most. Every modern large
language model, from GPT to Gemini to Llama, is a transformer trained on
massive amounts of text — this is why "language model" is often shortened
to "LLM."

Two important lessons come from this history: first, AI progress is a series
of hardware and algorithm step changes, not one continuous line; second, each
new era (symbols, rules, learning, transformers) solved a limitation of the
one before it.

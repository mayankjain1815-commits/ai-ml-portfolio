# AI in the Real World Today

Machine learning is already embedded in everyday products. Recommendation
systems power the feeds on video and music platforms, deciding what to show
next based on what you watched. Search engines rank pages using learned
models on top of classical indexing. Phones run small on-device models for
photo enhancement, text prediction, and face unlock.

Generative AI went mainstream in 2022 with the release of consumer chatbots
and image generators. Chatbots built on large language models now write
drafts of emails and code, explain concepts, and automate customer-support
triage. Image generators create marketing mockups, game concept art, and
educational diagrams in seconds.

In industry, the biggest wins are usually narrow and boring rather than
flashy. Predictive maintenance models forecast when factory machines will
fail, saving millions in downtime. Fraud-detection models score each
transaction in milliseconds and flag anomalies for human review. Demand
forecasting models help warehouses and grocery chains stock the right
amount tomorrow.

The practical pattern that repeats everywhere is human-in-the-loop: the
model proposes, ranks, or drafts; a person reviews, approves, and decides.
Systems that try to remove the human entirely, such as fully autonomous
moderation or self-driving taxis, run into long-tail problems — rare edge
cases that look obvious to a person but none of the training data covered.
